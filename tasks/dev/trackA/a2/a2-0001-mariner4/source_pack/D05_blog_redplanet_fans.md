# D05 — "The probe that proved Mars was dead" — Red Planet Fans blog

> Posted by MarsMike · 54 years ago today!

Everybody remembers Viking. Nobody remembers the little probe that got there first and basically ended the dream of Martian civilization in one afternoon.

Mariner 4 launched in late 1964 and reached Mars in July 1965, swinging within **5,000 miles** of the surface — closer than any spacecraft had ever come to another planet. It then **orbited Mars for several weeks**, snapping **22 photographs** with its onboard TV camera and beaming them back to Pasadena.

What came back was a shock. Craters. Nothing but craters, like the Moon. No canals, no vegetation, no cities. The atmosphere turned out to be so thin that liquid water couldn't exist. In one stroke, **Mariner 4 proved there was no life on Mars**, and the budget for Mars exploration collapsed for the rest of the decade.

Fun fact: the engineers at JPL were so impatient for the first image that they colored it in by hand with crayons while the computer was still crunching the numbers. That hand-colored picture still hangs at JPL today.

Mariner 4 kept transmitting until it ran out of power in **1966**. Not bad for a spacecraft built with less computing power than a modern toaster.
