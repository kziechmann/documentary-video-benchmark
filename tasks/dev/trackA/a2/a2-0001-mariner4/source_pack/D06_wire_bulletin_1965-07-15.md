# D06 — Wire bulletin: "Mariner 4 begins Mars picture playback"

**Source type:** contemporaneous news bulletin (reconstructed in period style for this benchmark) · **Dated:** 15 July 1965, 11:40 a.m. PDT · **Authority:** medium (contemporaneous, but written before playback was complete)

PASADENA, Calif., July 15 — The Mariner 4 spacecraft has begun transmitting its pictures of Mars back to Earth after a flight of 228 days, officials at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory said today.

The spacecraft passed the planet early this morning at a distance scientists put at roughly 6,000 miles. Its television camera **is expected to have recorded 22 pictures** during the 25-minute photographic pass, which are being read out from an onboard tape recorder at the rate of one picture every eight and a half hours.

Officials said the first picture, showing the edge of the planet against the blackness of space, is being assembled now. Because the data arrive as a stream of numbers, engineers said it would be some hours before a finished photograph could be released. In the meantime, members of the team were seen pinning paper strips of figures to a wall and shading them in by hand.

The complete set of pictures is not expected to be in hand before early August.
