## Mission overview

Mariner 4 was the fourth in a series of spacecraft used for planetary exploration in a flyby mode and the first to return close-range images of another planet. It was managed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory for NASA.

Launch: 28 November 1964, Cape Kennedy, Florida, Atlas-Agena D launch vehicle.

Mars flyby: 14–15 July 1965, closest approach 9,846 km at 01:00:57 UT on 15 July 1965.

End of mission: communications were terminated on 21 December 1967.

## Spacecraft

Launch mass 260.8 kg. Octagonal magnesium frame bus with four solar panels, high-gain and low-gain antennas, and a scan platform carrying a television camera. Attitude control referenced to the Sun and the star Canopus.

## Television experiment

A single vidicon television camera on the scan platform recorded images through alternating red and green filters. Each frame was 200 × 200 picture elements digitized to 6 bits (64 shades of gray). The pictures were recorded on an onboard tape recorder during the flyby and played back to Earth afterward.

Twenty-one complete pictures and 21 lines of a twenty-second were returned. The transmission rate was 8⅓ bits per second; a single picture required approximately 8 hours 35 minutes to transmit. Playback of the complete picture set was finished in early August 1965 (the set was transmitted twice to guard against errors).

The pictures covered a swath of roughly one percent of the planet's surface and revealed a cratered, lunar-like terrain. No evidence was seen of the linear "canals" reported by some earth-based observers.

## Occultation experiment

As the spacecraft passed behind Mars as seen from Earth, the refraction of its radio signal by the Martian atmosphere yielded a surface pressure in the range of approximately 4 to 7 millibars, far lower than most pre-encounter estimates (of order 85 millibars).

## Other experiments

Magnetometer, cosmic-ray telescope, cosmic dust detector, trapped-radiation detector, ionization chamber, and solar plasma probe. No Martian magnetic field or radiation belts were detected.
