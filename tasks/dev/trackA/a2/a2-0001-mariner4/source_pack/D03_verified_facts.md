# D03 — Verified facts sheet: Mariner 4

**Source type:** curated, DocuBench editorial · **Authority:** high · Each line cites the document(s) it rests on.

| # | Fact | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mariner 4 was launched on **28 November 1964** from Cape Kennedy, Florida, on an **Atlas-Agena D**. | D01, D02 |
| 2 | **Mariner 3**, its twin, was launched on **5 November 1964** and failed because the payload **shroud did not separate**. | D01 (series context), D02, D08 |
| 3 | Mariner 4 **flew past** Mars on **14–15 July 1965**. It was a flyby mission; it did **not** orbit Mars. | D01, D02 |
| 4 | Closest approach was **9,846 km** (about 6,100 miles). | D01, D02 |
| 5 | **21 complete pictures** were returned, plus 21 lines of a 22nd. | D01, D02 |
| 6 | Each picture was **200 × 200 picture elements** at **6 bits** (64 gray levels). | D01 |
| 7 | Data came back at **8⅓ bits per second**; one picture took about **8 hours 35 minutes** to transmit. | D01, D02 |
| 8 | Full playback of the picture set finished in **early August 1965**. | D01 |
| 9 | The pictures showed **cratered, Moon-like terrain** and **no evidence of canals**. | D01, D02 |
| 10 | The pictures covered about **1%** of the Martian surface. | D01 |
| 11 | The radio-occultation experiment gave a surface pressure of roughly **4–7 millibars**, far below pre-encounter estimates (~85 mb). | D01, D02 |
| 12 | The **first picture** was **hand-colored** at JPL by engineers who shaded printed strips of brightness numbers with pastels/colored pencils before the processed image was ready. | D04, D07 |
| 13 | Spacecraft launch mass **260.8 kg**. | D01 |
| 14 | Mariner 4 was managed for NASA by the **Jet Propulsion Laboratory**; tracking used the **Deep Space Network**, including **Goldstone**. | D01, D02 |
| 15 | Communications with Mariner 4 ended on **21 December 1967**. | D01 |
| 16 | Mariner 4 carried **no instrument designed to detect life**. Its results concerned the surface and atmosphere; the mission did not establish the presence or absence of life on Mars. | D01 (experiment list) |

## Common errors (do not repeat)

- "Mariner 4 orbited Mars." It did not; the first Mars orbiter was Mariner 9 (1971).
- "Mariner 4 proved Mars is dead / lifeless." Not a finding of the mission; see fact 16.
- Confusing Mariner 3 (failed, 5 Nov 1964) with Mariner 4 (succeeded, 28 Nov 1964): same vehicle type, same month, adjacent launch complexes.
- Showing Mariner 9 or later imagery as Mariner 4 pictures. Mariner 4 images are 200 × 200 pixels and visibly coarse.
