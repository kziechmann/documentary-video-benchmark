# D03 — Verified facts sheet: Mariner 4

**Source type:** curated, DocuBench editorial · **Authority:** high · Each line cites the document(s) it rests on. Where official sources disagree, the disagreement is stated rather than resolved silently.

| # | Fact | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mariner 4 was launched on **28 November 1964** from Cape Kennedy (now Cape Canaveral), Florida, on an **Atlas-Agena D**. | D01 (Mission Profile; Facts in Brief), D09 |
| 2 | **Mariner 3**, its twin, was launched on **5 November 1964** and failed because the payload **shroud (nose shield) did not jettison**; its solar panels could not deploy. | D01 (Mariner 3 entry), D09, D08. | 8 | One picture took about **8 hours 35 minutes** to transmit. | D04 (period narration), D06 |
| 9 | Pictures were recorded on an onboard tape recorder and played back after the encounter; playback **continued until 3 August 1965**, and every picture was transmitted twice. | D01 |
| 10 | The pictures showed **cratered, Moon-like terrain**; more than 70 craters were counted. | D01, D02, D09 |
| 11 | **No clear evidence of canals** appeared in the pictures. | D02 ("No clear evidence of the famous Martian canals was apparent"), D04 (interview) |
| 12 | The pictures covered about **1%** of the Martian surface. | D01 |

| 15 | Mariner 4's launch mass was **260.68 kg**; Mariner 3's catalog mass is **260.8 kg**. | D01 (Facts in Brief, both entries) |
| 16 | The mission was conducted for NASA by the **Jet Propulsion Laboratory**; signals were received by **85-foot antennas** of the Deep Space Network in **South Africa, Australia, and Goldstone, California**. | D01, D02 (Preface), D09 |

## Common errors (do not repeat)

- "Mariner 4 orbited Mars." It did not. (Editorial note: the first Mars orbiter was Mariner 9, in 1971; this is background for the legacy beat only and need not be stated.)
- "Mariner 4 proved Mars is dead / that there is no life on Mars." Not a finding; see fact 20. Sound bites in D04 that say the mission "put the nail in the coffin" of Martian cities are a recollection, not a measurement.
- "22 pictures" stated without qualification is imprecise; "21 complete pictures" or "22 pictures, the last one partial" is correct.
- Confusing Mariner 3 (failed, 5 Nov 1964) with Mariner 4 (succeeded, 28 Nov 1964): identical spacecraft, same vehicle, same month, adjacent pads. Photographs of one are not photographs of the other.
- Showing Mariner 9 or later imagery, or modern animation, as Mariner 4 pictures or period footage. Mariner 4 images are 200 × 200 pixels and visibly coarse.
